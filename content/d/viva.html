Viva (execviva.com): AI and search visibility

US market, English. AI answers measured November 13, 2026; Google AI Overviews November 14, 2026. Compared with Athena (athena.com), BELAY (belaysolutions.com) and Prialto (prialto.com).

When a US buyer asks ChatGPT which virtual executive assistant service to hire, ChatGPT names Viva in 3 of 219 answers and BELAY in 180. Gemini does better for Viva (27 of 224), and Google's AI Overviews name Viva in 1 of 20 category searches. The engines already read execviva.com (it is cited as a source in 49 AI answers), but they do not recommend the brand. Two causes stand out: "Viva" is read as Microsoft Viva or a European bank, and the comparison and "best of" pages the engines quote list competitors, not Viva.

6. How every number in this report was produced

Engines and how they were asked
ChatGPT and Gemini were queried through DataForSEO's LLM Scraper, which runs the question in each product's consumer interface and returns what a person would see, including the sources shown. ChatGPT was run with web search forced on. Location United States, language English. Claude, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and Google AI Mode were not measured.
The questions
25 questions built from the buyer phrase "virtual executive assistant services": 7 shortlist questions ("best", "top for a startup"), 7 comparison questions, 3 location questions, 7 buying-problem questions (cost, what to ask, what goes wrong) and 1 question asking what Viva is. The full list is in the question-by-question table in section 1. Questions never contain Viva's name, except the last one.
Why each question was asked 10 times
AI answers are not stable. The same question asked twice, minutes apart, returns a different set of companies: in our own measurements two identical runs share only about 36% of the brands they name. One answer is an anecdote; ten answers per question per engine give a rate.
What "named" means
A company counts as named in an answer when its name or its domain appears in the text, found by exact text matching on the stored answer, not by asking a model to judge. Because "Viva" is an ordinary word and the name of other companies, mentions of Viva are flagged when the surrounding text does not confirm it is the executive assistant company (its domain or category words nearby). All 30 detections are counted above; on ChatGPT 2 of the 3 are confirmed by context, on Gemini 14 of 27.
What 443, 219 and 224 are
500 answers were collected. 37 named no business at all (mostly general advice) and 20 answered the "What is Viva" question. The remaining 443 answers to buyer questions that named at least one business are the base: 219 on ChatGPT and 224 on Gemini.
Sources cited
The links each engine displayed as sources with its answer, counted once per answer per domain, over the 463 answers that came back with content.
Google AI Overviews
Google US desktop results via DataForSEO's SERP API, with Google's asynchronously loaded AI Overviews requested, on November 14. For each search we recorded whether an AI Overview appeared, the sites it cites, and which services it names in its text (exact text matching against the services listed in the chart). AI Overviews change from one search to the next: two runs minutes apart returned an overview on 26 and on 27 of the 36 terms. Treat this section as one sample, not a fixed property of each term.
Estimated monthly organic visits
DataForSEO's Estimated Traffic Volume. For every keyword where the domain appears in Google's organic results, it multiplies the keyword's monthly search volume by the expected click-through rate of the domain's position, and adds them up. The expected click-through rate depends on the position, the search intent (informational or commercial) and what else is on the page: an AI Overview, ads or a map pack take clicks away from organic results. Search volume is Google's, refined with clickstream data where available. DataForSEO made this model the default on November 1, 2026. It is an estimate built the same way for every domain, which makes it good for comparing Viva with its competitors and not a substitute for Viva's own analytics or Google Search Console, which measure real visits.
Sources: How ETV is calculated, New ETV calculation.
Keywords a domain ranks for
The number of keywords in DataForSEO's US Google database for which the domain appears anywhere in the top 100 organic results.
Live Google check
36 terms chosen by us (category, competitor and brand terms), searched on Google US desktop on November 13, first page (top 10 organic results). Position is Viva's rank among organic results; "not in top 10" means it was not on the first page.
Where execviva.com gets its traffic
DataForSEO's ranking database for the domain, sorted by estimated visits per keyword (search volume times expected click-through rate, as above).
Keyword ideas, search volume, cost per click, competition
DataForSEO Labs keyword ideas seeded with category terms. Monthly searches are Google's average for the US. Cost per click is what advertisers pay on average in Google Ads. Competition (0 to 1) is how crowded the paid results are, not how hard the organic ranking is.
Site audit
DataForSEO's On-Page crawler started at execviva.com and followed internal links, finding 487 pages. Each check counts the pages where an issue was found. The audit flags issues; it does not rank them by business impact, which is why the table explains each one.
Backlinks, referring domains and rank
From DataForSEO's backlink index. Backlinks are individual links; referring domains are the distinct websites they come from (subdomains counted separately); referring main domains merge subdomains. Domain rank (0 to 1000) is DataForSEO's PageRank-style score: it applies Google's original PageRank formula to its own link graph, where a link from a page that is itself well linked counts more, and a domain's rank is the normalized sum of its pages' ranks. Spam score (0 to 100) estimates how much of the link profile comes from spammy sources.
Source: What is Rank in Backlinks API.
Link gap
DataForSEO's domain intersection: websites in the backlink index that link to at least one of athena.com, belaysolutions.com or prialto.com and do not link to execviva.com. The total (531) counts all of them; the table keeps only sites with a rank above 0 that are not link directories or backlink shops.
AI mention index and AI search volume
DataForSEO's LLM Mentions database: AI answers DataForSEO collects on its own schedule (ChatGPT, US English only; and Google AI Overviews). "Mentions" is how many answers in that database mention the domain. "AI search volume" estimates how often the underlying questions are asked: for Google it is derived from Google search volume; for ChatGPT it uses DataForSEO's own model based on Google's "People also ask" data. DataForSEO does not publish how its answers are collected or refreshed, so use this index as a second opinion next to our own measurement in section 1, which we control end to end.
Source: How AI search volume works.
Why numbers differ between sections
Each section measures a different thing on a different date with a different method: live answers we collected (section 1), a live Google sample (section 2), DataForSEO's databases (sections 3 to 5). They agree on the direction: competitors are named and linked far more than Viva.

7. What we would do first

  1. Disambiguate the name. Use "Viva Executive Assistants" or "ExecViva" consistently on the site, in structured data (Organization schema with the domain, founding, category) and on every profile, so "Viva" stops resolving to Microsoft Viva.
  2. Get onto the pages the engines quote. The answers lean on comparison and "best virtual assistant companies" pages, Reddit threads and Forbes lists. Start with the link gap sites and the sources in section 1.
  3. Own the comparison searches. "belay alternatives", "athena alternatives", "viva vs belay" and "viva vs athena" have no Viva page on Google's first page. Pages that answer those questions honestly are what both Google and AI engines quote.
  4. Answer the buying questions. Cost, what to ask before hiring, and what goes wrong are asked often and answered with no company named. Clear, specific pages on those questions are an opening.
  5. Fix the audit basics. Duplicate titles, missing alt text and links through redirects are cheap to fix and help every crawler.
